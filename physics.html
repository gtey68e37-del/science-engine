Physics Tool Guide: Force, Momentum & Kinetic Energy

Newton's Second Law (F = m × a) • Momentum (p = m × v) • Kinetic Energy (KE = ½ m v²)

More Information

The Physics tool in Apex Science Engine connects Newton's Second Law with motion quantities starting from mass m (in kilograms, kg) and acceleration a (in meters per second squared, m/s²). In Step 1 (Net Force), it multiplies mass by acceleration using F = m × a to find the net force in Newtons (N). For example, with m = 12 kg and a = 9.8 m/s², the net force is F = 12 × 9.8 = 117.60 N[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).

Force: F = m × a (N)  |  Momentum: p = m × v (kg·m/s)  |  Kinetic Energy: KE = ½ m v² (J)

In Step 2 (Momentum), the tool calculates linear momentum p using p = m × v, where v is velocity in m/s and momentum is measured in kg·m/s[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span). Momentum measures how hard it is to stop a moving object: doubling either the mass m or the velocity v doubles the momentum p, assuming the other stays constant.

In Step 3 (Kinetic Energy), the tool computes the energy of motion using KE = ½ m v², measured in Joules (J)[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span). Notice the critical difference between momentum and kinetic energy: velocity v is squared (v²) in kinetic energy! That means if velocity v doubles, momentum doubles (2×), while kinetic energy increases by four times (2² = 4×) for the same mass m.

When working through physics calculations, common mistakes include confusing mass (kg) with force (N), forgetting to square velocity v in KE = ½ m v², or forgetting the ½ factor in front of m v². Always check units (N for force, kg·m/s for momentum, and J for kinetic energy) to verify your result makes physical sense.

Quick Check

1. In Step 1 (F = m × a), what is the net force for m = 10 kg and a = 4 m/s²?

2. In Step 2 (p = m × v), what is the momentum of a 6 kg object moving at v = 5 m/s?

3. In Step 3 (KE = ½ m v²), if velocity v doubles while mass m stays the same, what happens to KE?
