Physics Motion Tool Guide: Uniform Acceleration Kinematics

Initial Velocity (v₀) • Final Velocity (v = v₀ + at) • Distance (d = v₀t + ½at²)

More Information

The Physics Motion tool in Apex Science Engine applies kinematics equations for motion under constant (uniform) acceleration using three inputs: initial velocity (v₀ in m/s), acceleration (a in m/s²), and time (t in seconds)[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span). With a constant acceleration, velocity changes at a steady rate over time rather than jumping randomly[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).

Final Velocity: v = v₀ + (a × t)  |  Distance: d = (v₀ × t) + (½ × a × t²)

To find Final Velocity (v), the tool uses v = v₀ + at[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span). For example, starting from v₀ = 0 m/s with acceleration a = 9.8 m/s² for t = 5 seconds gives v = 0 + (9.8 × 5) = 49.00 m/s[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span). Every additional second of acceleration adds another 9.8 m/s to the speed.

To find Distance (d), the tool uses d = v₀t + ½at²[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span). Using the same example (v₀ = 0, a = 9.8 m/s², t = 5 s), d = 0 + 0.5 × 9.8 × (5)² = 4.9 × 25 = 122.50 meters[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span). Notice that time t is squared in the acceleration term (½at²), so doubling time increases the distance covered by that acceleration term fourfold[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span).

Common mistakes in kinematics problems include squaring time t inside v₀t when only ½at² has t², forgetting the ½ factor when computing distance, or mixing up units such as meters (m) for distance and meters per second (m/s) for velocity[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span). Always evaluate exponents (t²) before multiplying by ½ and a.
